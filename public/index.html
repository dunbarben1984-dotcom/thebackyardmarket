Built for how a local market actually works

A few things worth adding beyond the basics — these are the features that tend to make or break a two-sided marketplace like this one.

Verified farm badges

Farmers can upload a certification (USDA Organic, Certified Naturally Grown, etc.) that shows as a badge on listings — builds trust without you policing every claim.

Seasonal availability

Listings can be marked "in season," "pre-order," or "sold out for the year" so buyers aren't disappointed and farmers aren't fielding the same question weekly.

In-app messaging

Buyers and farmers coordinate pickup times and quantities without exchanging phone numbers up front.

Map & distance search

"Show me everything within 15 miles" matters more here than almost any other filter — worth building before most other features.

CSA / subscription boxes

Let farmers offer a recurring weekly box in addition to one-off listings — a second revenue stream for them, and a reason for buyers to keep coming back.

Ratings & repeat-buyer history

Simple 5-star reviews after pickup, plus a "bought again" signal — the thing that makes people trust a stranger's tomatoes.

Market & pop-up calendar

Farmers can list physical market dates/locations too, not just standing listings — useful even for farmers who mainly still sell in person.

Bulk / wholesale tier

A toggle for restaurants or co-ops buying in volume, with quantity-based pricing — a natural upsell once you have both sides established.

Waitlist for sold-out items

"Notify me when more grass-fed beef is available" keeps demand visible to farmers instead of just vanishing.

Simple annual pricing

One flat fee, no commission on sales — farmers keep 100% of what they price their goods at.
